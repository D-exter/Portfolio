About the game

Detective Dexter and the Parade Pooper is a 2D Metroidvania action platformer built in Unity during Year 3 Semester 1. The game is themed around SG60: Detective Dexter, a foreign agent, arrives in Singapore to investigate Vector Three, who is attempting to shut down the country's power during the National Day Parade. Players explore Singapore-inspired environments, collect cultural items like kopi and durians, and encounter references to local folklore, all while fighting to keep the NDP going.

Following Metroidvania conventions, the game features non-linear exploration, power-up unlocks that gate new areas, and multi-stage boss fights, layered over a modular component architecture built to support rapid iteration across a two-week sprint.

What I built

Built the full player controller covering basic movement (run and jump), directional attacking including wall slide attacks, and a health system with knockback, i-frames, screen shake, and time-freeze visual feedback.
Programmed a Finite State Machine governing all movement skills: dash, wall slide, wall jump, double jump, and pogo, each only enabled once the corresponding upgrade is collected.
Designed an event-driven ability and items system that parses unlock states to extend player navigation and map access when items are picked up, including artifact effects such as longer attacks, attack speed, shorter dash cooldown, and increased health.
Implemented seamless scene transitions in all directions (left to right, up to down) alongside a checkpoint respawn system using flags and a fast travel system via MRT stations.
Engineered custom 2D hitbox and hurtbox layer filters for precise hit registration across player attacks, weapon swings, and environmental hazards such as barbed wire and spiky bushes, with logic to return the player to a safe point after contact.
Constructed the full menu and UI suite: main menu, pause menu, volume settings, and a player health HUD, alongside a centralised SFX player and particle effect manager.
Implemented camera follow logic tuned for falling movement, and designed the tutorial scenes guiding players through the opening parade area.

Challenges & Solutions

Challenge: Meeting the deadline while managing the volume of planned content, as the scope was more ambitious than previous WIUs with a much tighter timeline.
Solution: Prioritised core gameplay features and cut less essential content that would not significantly impact the overall gameplay experience.
Challenge: The higher complexity of this project led to more bugs and semantic errors, which consumed significant debugging time and caused delays.
Solution: Worked more closely as a team by regularly reviewing and playtesting new features to catch errors earlier and reduce debugging overhead.

Outcome

Delivered a complete, playable Metroidvania across nine interconnected scenes within two weeks. The modular player FSM and unlock system allowed the team to iterate on level design and encounter parameters rapidly, while the centralised UI, SFX, and transition systems kept integration clean across all four contributors.
